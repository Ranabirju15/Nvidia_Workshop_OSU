Requirements for MD Integrators

MD integrators must solve Newton's equations: m d²r/dt² = F(r). Key requirements:

  1. Time-reversibility — The exact dynamics is reversible; the integrator should be too.
  2. Symplecticity — Preserves phase space volume (Liouville's theorem); conserves energy long-term.
  3. Energy conservation — In NVE, total energy should fluctuate around a constant (no drift).
  4. Large stable time step — To maximize sampling efficiency.
  5. Computational efficiency — Minimal force evaluations per step.

The Verlet and Velocity Verlet algorithms are the gold standard — symplectic, time-reversible, and require only 1 force evaluation per step.

Verlet Algorithm

Position Verlet (from Taylor expansion): 

r(t+Δt) = 2r(t) − r(t−Δt) + a(t)Δt² + O(Δt⁴)

Velocities are not directly available (can be computed as v(t) = [r(t+Δt)−r(t−Δt)]/(2Δt)). Velocity Verlet (preferred) gives positions and velocities at the same time: 

r(t+Δt) = r(t) + v(t)Δt + ½ a(t)Δt²
v(t+Δt) = v(t) + ½ [a(t) + a(t+Δt)] Δt

Algorithm: (1) r(t+Δt) from r(t), v(t), a(t). (2) Compute a(t+Δt) from r(t+Δt). (3) v(t+Δt) from v(t), a(t), a(t+Δt). Exactly 1 force evaluation per step.

Leapfrog & Beeman

Leapfrog (velocity at half-steps): 

v(t+½Δt) = v(t−½Δt) + a(t)Δt
r(t+Δt) = r(t) + v(t+½Δt)Δt

Equivalent to Velocity Verlet but velocities are offset. Beeman algorithm (for comparison): 

r(t+Δt) = r(t) + v(t)Δt + (4a(t)−a(t−Δt))Δt²/6
v(t+Δt) = v(t) + (5a(t+Δt)+8a(t)−a(t−Δt))Δt/12

Beeman is higher order but requires storing previous acceleration; not symplectic. Rarely used in modern MD.

Multiple Time Step (MTS) Methods

Forces separate into fast (bonded) and slow (nonbonded). MTS evaluates fast forces every step, slow forces every k steps. r-RESPA (reversible reference system propagator algorithm): 

F = Ffast + Fslow

Propagator: exp(Δt L) ≈ [exp(Δt/2 Lslow) exp(Δt/2 Lfast) exp(Δt Lfast) exp(Δt/2 Lfast) exp(Δt/2 Lslow)]k

Typical: bonded every 1 fs, short-range nonbonded every 2 fs, PME every 4–6 fs. MTS can give 2–3× speedup but risks resonance artifacts if not tuned carefully.

Constraints: SHAKE, LINCS, SETTLE

Constraining bonds involving hydrogen (or all bonds) allows 2–4 fs time steps by removing fast vibrations.

  • SHAKE — Iterative constraint solver (Gaussian elimination for small clusters). Converges to tolerance; sequential.
  • LINCS — Linear constraint solver (analytical inversion of constraint matrix). Faster, parallelizable; standard in GROMACS.
  • SETTLE — Analytical solution for rigid water (3 atoms, 3 constraints). Exact, extremely fast.

Constraints modify the phase space measure; the partition function includes a factor ∏ (1/mi)1/2. This contributes to the free energy (important for TI/FEP). Velocity Verlet with constraints (RATTLE) is symplectic.

Error Analysis & Time Step Selection

Local truncation error per step: O(Δt³) for positions, O(Δt²) for velocities (Velocity Verlet). Global error after time T: O(Δt²). Energy drift in NVE is O(Δt²) for symplectic integrators.

Hydrogen Mass Repartitioning (HMR) shifts mass from heavy atoms to hydrogens, slowing fastest modes. Allows 4 fs with constrained H-bonds. Validated for many properties but affects kinetics.
