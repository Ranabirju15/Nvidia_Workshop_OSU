The Sampling Problem

Standard MD is limited by ergodicity breaking: high free energy barriers trap the system in metastable states. Timescales for barrier crossing: τ ~ τ0 exp(ΔF/kBT). For ΔF = 10 kBT, τ ~ 10⁴ τ0; for ΔF = 20 kBT, τ ~ 10⁸ τ0. With τ0 ~ ps, this means μs–s barriers are inaccessible to plain MD.

Enhanced sampling methods accelerate barrier crossing while preserving (or allowing reweighting to) the correct Boltzmann distribution. They fall into several categories:

Collective Variable (CV) Based Methods

These bias the simulation along predefined collective variables ξ(r).

  • Umbrella Sampling: Harmonic restraints at windows along ξ. WHAM/MBAR combines windows. Rigorous but requires knowing ξ and window spacing.
  • Metadynamics: History-dependent bias V(ξ,t) = Σ W exp(−(ξ−ξ(t'))²/2σ²). Fills free energy wells; converges to −F(ξ). Well-tempered metadynamics (WTMetaD) controls bias deposition for smooth convergence.
  • Adaptive Biasing Force (ABF): Estimates mean force ⟨−∇ξU⟩ξ on-the-fly and applies counteracting bias. No Gaussians; direct force estimate.
  • Steered MD / Jarzynski: Pull along ξ at constant velocity. Non-equilibrium work gives ΔF via Jarzynski equality: ΔF = −kBT ln ⟨exp(−βW)⟩. Requires many repeats.

Temperature-Based Methods

These modify the temperature to enhance barrier crossing.

  • Replica Exchange MD (REMD / T-REMD): M replicas at T1 < T2 < ... < TM. Exchanges attempted every τex with Metropolis criterion: Pacc = min(1, exp[(βi−βj)(Uj−Ui)]). High-T replicas cross barriers; low-T replicas sample correctly. Scales poorly with system size (need ~√N replicas for constant acceptance).
  • Hamiltonian REMD (H-REMD): Replicas with different Hamiltonians (e.g., scaled interactions, soft-core alchemical). Better scaling for large systems.
  • Simulated Tempering: Single replica with dynamic T; T is a dynamical variable with expanded ensemble weights. No replica overhead but harder to tune.
  • REST2 (Replica Exchange with Solute Tempering): Only solute temperature scaled; solvent at 300 K. Far fewer replicas needed for biomolecules.

Alchemical & Free Energy Methods

These compute free energy differences by transforming the Hamiltonian.

  • Thermodynamic Integration (TI): ΔG = ∫01 ⟨∂H/∂λ⟩λ dλ. Requires multiple λ-windows; soft-core potentials avoid singularities.
  • Free Energy Perturbation (FEP): ΔG = −kBT ln ⟨exp(−βΔU)⟩. BAR/MBAR use forward/reverse work for optimal estimator.
  • λ-Dynamics / Continuous Constant pH: λ becomes a dynamical variable with fictitious mass. Samples λ continuously; extended ensemble weights give correct distribution.
  • Enveloping Distribution Sampling (EDS): Single reference state envelops multiple end states. One simulation yields multiple ΔG values.

Path Sampling & Rare Events

For kinetics and transition paths:

  • Transition Path Sampling (TPS): Monte Carlo in trajectory space. Harvests reactive trajectories without predefined CV.
  • Transition Interface Sampling (TIS): Interfaces along path; computes rate constant and mechanism. More efficient than TPS.
  • Milestoning: Divide space into milestones; compute local kinetics. Assembles global rates from short trajectories.
  • Weighted Ensemble: Parallel trajectories with splitting/merging to maintain uniform sampling in progress coordinate. Exact kinetics.

Machine Learning Enhanced Sampling

Deep Learning CVs: VAE, TICA, or autoencoders learn slow collective variables from data. Neural Network Bias: NN learns bias potential (e.g., DeepCV, Reinforcement Learning). Active Learning: Iterative sampling + model retraining (e.g., for reaction coordinates).

Caution: ML methods require validation; they can introduce bias if the model is not flexible enough or overfits. Always verify with independent methods.
