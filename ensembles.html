Ensemble Classification

MD simulations target specific thermodynamic ensembles. The choice depends on the physical system and properties of interest:

EnsembleFixedFluctuatingPartition FunctionMD Method
Microcanonical (NVE)N, V, ET, PΩ(N,V,E)Native (Verlet)
Canonical (NVT)N, V, TE, PQ(N,V,T)Thermostat
Isothermal-isobaric (NPT)N, P, TE, VΔ(N,P,T)Thermostat + Barostat

NVE: The Native MD Ensemble

Standard Verlet integration conserves energy exactly (up to numerical error). NVE is the only ensemble requiring no thermostat/barostat. It is used for:

  • Testing energy conservation (validates integrator, forces, time step)
  • Microcanonical rate constants (RRKM theory)
  • Reference for comparing thermostat artifacts

Caution: Real systems exchange energy with surroundings. NVE simulations of small systems show large T fluctuations (ΔT/T ~ 1/√N). For biomolecules, NVE is rarely used for production.

NVT: Constant Temperature

The canonical ensemble is the workhorse for most biomolecular simulations. It corresponds to a system in thermal equilibrium with a large heat bath. Key properties:

  • Temperature is fixed; energy fluctuates with variance ⟨(ΔE)²⟩ = kBT²CV.
  • Density is fixed (volume constant); pressure fluctuates.
  • Correct for comparing to experiments at constant T (most lab conditions).

NVT is also used for free energy calculations (FEP, TI) where volume changes would complicate the alchemical path.

NPT: Constant Pressure & Temperature

The isothermal-isobaric ensemble matches most experimental conditions (1 atm, 298 K). Volume fluctuates: 

⟨(ΔV)²⟩ = kBT κT V

NPT is essential for:

  • Equilibrating density (e.g., new force field, new system)
  • Simulating phase transitions, pressure-dependent phenomena
  • Computing enthalpy H = U + PV and heat capacity CP
  • Obtaining correct box dimensions for subsequent NVT production

Barostat choice matters: isotropic (cubic box) vs. anisotropic (triclinic, membrane normal). Membranes require semi-isotropic (xy-coupled, z-independent) or surface tension control.

Advanced Ensembles

μVT (Grand Canonical): Particle number fluctuates at fixed chemical potential μ. Implemented via GCMC moves (insertion/deletion) or λ-dynamics. Essential for: adsorption in porous materials, ion concentration equilibration, phase equilibria.

Replica Exchange (REMD): Multiple replicas at different T (or λ, pH) exchange configurations. Samples rugged free energy landscapes. Not a single ensemble but a sampling strategy.

Constant pH MD: Protonation states change dynamically via λ-dynamics or Monte Carlo. Couples to solution pH. Important for titratable residues in proteins.

Ensemble Equivalence & Finite-Size Effects

In the thermodynamic limit (N→∞), ensembles are equivalent: fluctuations scale as 1/√N. For finite N (typical MD: N ~ 10⁴–10⁵), differences exist: 

CP − CV = T V αP² / κT    (exact)

In NVT, CV is measured; in NPT, CP. For water at 300 K, CP ≈ 75 J/mol·K, CV ≈ 65 J/mol·K — a ~15% difference. Always report which ensemble was used.

Finite-size pressure fluctuations: In NPT, ⟨(ΔP)²⟩ ~ 1/V. Small boxes have huge pressure noise (100s of bar), but this does not invalidate the ensemble — it's a feature, not a bug.
