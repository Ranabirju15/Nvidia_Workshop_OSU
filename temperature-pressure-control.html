Thermostats: Generating the Canonical Ensemble

A thermostat modifies the equations of motion to sample the NVT ensemble. Two main classes:

  • Stochastic — Couple to a heat bath via random forces (Langevin, Andersen).
  • Deterministic — Extend phase space with thermostat variables (Nose–Hoover, CSVR).

Desired properties: Correct canonical distribution, minimal perturbation of dynamics, fast equilibration, ergodicity, computational efficiency.

Stochastic Thermostats

Langevin dynamics adds friction and random force: 

m dv/dt = F(r) − γ m v + R(t)
⟨R(t)⟩ = 0,    ⟨R(t)R(t')⟩ = 2 γ m kBT δ(t−t')

γ (collision frequency) controls coupling strength. γ⁻¹ ~ 1–10 ps typical. Samples correct NVT for any γ > 0. Damps high-frequency motions; alters kinetics/diffusion. Good for equilibration, implicit solvent, and systems with poor energy conservation.

Andersen thermostat — Randomly reassign velocities from Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution with collision frequency ν. ν⁻¹ ~ 0.1–1 ps. Strongly perturbs dynamics; excellent for equilibration.

Deterministic Thermostats

Nose–Hoover (NH) extends phase space with a thermostat variable s and "mass" Q: 

dr/dt = p/m,    dp/dt = F − p s
ds/dt = (p²/m − kBT) / Q

Q controls response time; Q = 3N kBT τ². Samples correct NVT if ergodic. Problem: Non-ergodic for small/harmonic systems (e.g., single oscillator).

Nose–Hoover Chains (NHC) — Chain of thermostats thermostatting each other. Restores ergodicity. Standard in many codes (LAMMPS, GROMACS). 3–4 chains typical.

CSVR (Canonical Sampling through Velocity Rescaling) — Velocity rescaling with stochastic term to generate correct fluctuations. Gentle, ergodic, minimal dynamics perturbation.

Barostats: Generating the Isobaric Ensemble

A barostat couples the system to a pressure bath by scaling coordinates and box vectors.

Berendsen barostat (weak coupling): scales box exponentially toward target pressure. Not a true NPT ensemble — suppresses fluctuations. Use only for equilibration. 

dV/dt = (V/τP) (Ptarget − Pinst)

Parrinello–Rahman (PR) — Extended Lagrangian with box matrix h as dynamical variables. Correct NPT ensemble. Allows anisotropic cell fluctuations (shape changes). 

d²h/dt² = (V/W) (Pinst − Pext) h−T

W = "mass" of box degrees of freedom. Standard in GROMACS, LAMMPS.

MTTK (Martyna–Tuckerman–Tobias–Klein) — Modern formulation of Nose–Hoover + PR with correct phase space measure. The gold standard for NPT. Used in GROMACS (mtk), NAMD, LAMMPS.

Key rules: (1) Never use Berendsen for production. (2) τP > τT (pressure relaxes slower). (3) For membranes: semi-isotropic (xy-coupled) or surface tension. (4) Avoid coupling solute and solvent separately unless needed (e.g., hot solvent/cold solute).

Artifacts & Pitfalls

  • Flying ice cube: Zero-frequency mode (center-of-mass motion) steals energy. Fix: remove COM motion periodically (standard in all MD codes).
  • Thermostat-induced artifacts: Langevin damps diffusion; NHC can have slow energy drift if Q poorly chosen; CSVR is safest for dynamics.
  • Barostat artifacts: Anisotropic PR/MTTK can cause box shape instability (especially vacuum gaps). Use semi-isotropic for membranes.
  • Dual thermostats: Separate solute/solvent thermostats can cause "hot solvent/cold solute" non-equilibrium. Use single thermostat unless justified.
  • Pressure calculation: Virial pressure has large fluctuations (100s bar). Average over ps–ns; instantaneous pressure is meaningless.
