Phase Space & Ensembles

A classical system of N particles in 3D has a 6N-dimensional phase space Γ = {r1,...,rN, p1,...,pN}. A microstate is a single point in Γ. An ensemble is a probability distribution ρ(Γ) over phase space.

The fundamental postulate: an isolated system in equilibrium explores all accessible microstates with equal probability. This defines the microcanonical ensemble (NVE): 

ρNVE(Γ) = δ(E − H(Γ)) / Ω(N,V,E)

where H = K + U is the Hamiltonian, Ω = ∫ δ(E−H) dΓ is the density of states, and entropy S = kB ln Ω.

Canonical & Isobaric Ensembles

The canonical ensemble (NVT) describes a system in thermal contact with a heat bath at temperature T: 

ρNVT(Γ) = exp(−βH(Γ)) / Q(N,V,T)
Q = ∫ exp(−βH(Γ)) dΓ    (partition function)

The isothermal-isobaric ensemble (NPT) adds a volume fluctuations with a pressure bath: 

ρNPT(Γ,V) = exp(−β(H(Γ) + PV)) / Δ(N,P,T)
Δ = ∫∫ exp(−β(H+PV)) dΓ dV

Other ensembles: μVT (grand canonical), μPT, etc. MD naturally generates NVE; NVT/NPT require thermostats/barostats.

Ergodicity & Time Averages

The ergodic hypothesis: for an ergodic system, the time average of an observable A equals its ensemble average: 

⟨A⟩ensemble = ∫ A(Γ) ρ(Γ) dΓ = limτ→∞ (1/τ) ∫0τ A(Γ(t)) dt = ⟨A⟩time

MD relies on this to compute thermodynamic properties from a single trajectory. Ergodicity breaking occurs when the system is trapped in a subset of phase space (e.g., separated by high free energy barriers).

Fluctuation–Dissipation & Response

In NVT, fluctuations relate to thermodynamic derivatives: 

⟨(ΔE)²⟩ = kBT² CV    ⟨(ΔN)²⟩ = kBT (∂N/∂μ)V,T
⟨(ΔV)²⟩ = kBT κT V    (in NPT)

The fluctuation–dissipation theorem connects equilibrium fluctuations to linear response: χAB(t) = β ⟨A(0)B(t)⟩ (for canonical variables). This underlies transport coefficient calculations (Green–Kubo relations).

Free Energy & Potential of Mean Force

The potential of mean force (PMF) along a reaction coordinate ξ is: 

W(ξ) = −kBT ln P(ξ) + const

where P(ξ) is the marginal probability density of ξ. The PMF is the free energy profile; barriers give kinetics via Kramers' theory. Free energy differences between states A and B: 

ΔF = −kBT ln ⟨ exp(−βΔU) ⟩A    (Zwanzig/FEP)
ΔF = ∫01 ⟨ ∂H/∂λ ⟩λ dλ    (Thermodynamic Integration)

These are the basis for alchemical free energy calculations.
