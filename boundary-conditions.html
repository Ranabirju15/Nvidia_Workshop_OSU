Periodic Boundary Conditions (PBC)

PBC replicate the simulation box infinitely in all directions to eliminate surface effects. Each particle interacts with the nearest periodic image of every other particle (minimum image convention). 

rij = rj − ri − L round(rj − ri) / L

For triclinic boxes, the box matrix h transforms fractional to Cartesian coordinates. The minimum image is found in fractional coordinates then transformed back.

Requirement: Cutoff rc ≤ L/2 (cubic) or rc ≤ min(hi/2) to avoid self-interaction. Violation causes artifacts.

Rhombic dodecahedron and truncated octahedron are space-filling polyhedra that approximate a sphere. They reduce water count by ~25–30% for a given solute–box distance. Most MD codes support them (GROMACS: -bt dodecahedron/octahedron).

Long-Range Electrostatics: Ewald & PME

The Coulomb sum in periodic systems is conditionally convergent. Ewald summation splits it: 

U = ½ Σi≠j qiqj erfc(α rij)/rij  +  ½ (1/πV) Σk≠0 (4π/k²) |Σ qj exp(−ik·rj)|² exp(−k²/4α²)  −  α/√π Σ qi²

Real space (short-range): erfc damping, cutoff ~1.0 nm. Reciprocal space: Fourier sum over k-vectors. Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) interpolates charges onto a grid and uses FFT: O(N log N).

PME Parameters: Grid spacing ~0.12 nm (≤ 0.15 nm), spline order 4–6, real-space cutoff 1.0–1.2 nm, α chosen so erfc(α rc) ~ 10⁻⁵. Force accuracy ~10⁻⁴ kJ/mol/nm. Always verify energy conservation in NVE with PME.

Lennard-Jones: Cutoffs, Switching & Dispersion Correction

LJ potential is short-range but truncation causes energy/pressure errors.

  • Plain cutoff — Discontinuous force at rc; energy drift. Obsolete.
  • Force switch — Smooth force to zero over [ron, rc]. Better.
  • Potential switch — Smooth potential to zero. Force not zero at rc; small impulse.
  • Long-range dispersion correction — Analytical tail correction assuming uniform density beyond rc: 
    ΔU = (8π/3) ρ ε σ³ [ (σ/rc)⁹/3 − (σ/rc)³ ]    (per particle)
    Essential for correct pressure and surface tension.

Standard: rc = 1.0–1.2 nm, force switch from 0.9–1.0 nm, dispersion correction ON. For lipid bilayers/membranes, use larger cutoffs (1.4 nm) or LJ-PME.

Artifacts of PBC & Finite Size

  • Periodic image interactions: Protein interacts with its own image if box too small. Rule: solute–box distance ≥ 1.0–1.2 nm (2× cutoff).
  • Artificial correlation: Collective motions (e.g., domain motions) couple to box size. Test by doubling box size.
  • Electrostatic finite-size effects: Charged systems in PBC have a net neutralizing background. Free energy of charging depends on box size (Born solvation artifact). Correct with finite-size corrections (e.g., Rocklin et al. for FEP).
  • Hydrodynamic artifacts: PBC alters diffusion (D ∝ 1/L). Correction: D(∞) = D(L) + 2.837 kBT / (6πηL).
  • Pressure tensor anisotropy: In NPT, PBC forces isotropic pressure; real interfaces have surface tension.

Non-Periodic & Hybrid Boundaries

Stochastic Boundary / Solvent Boundary: Inner region (active) MD, outer region Langevin/stochastic. Used for QM/MM boundaries, enzyme active sites.

Implicit solvent (GB/SA, PB): No explicit water; boundary is the solute surface. Fast but loses explicit water effects (hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic effect).

Slab/2D PBC: Periodic in xy, non-periodic in z (with vacuum or explicit interface). For membranes, interfaces. Requires Ewald 2D correction (e.g., Ewald3D with large z-spacing + correction).
