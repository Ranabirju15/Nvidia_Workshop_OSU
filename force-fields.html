Force Field Functional Form

A classical force field expresses the potential energy U as a sum of bonded and nonbonded terms: 

U = Ubond + Uangle + Udihedral + Uimproper + Unonbonded
Ubond = Σbonds ½ kb (r − r0)²
Uangle = Σangles ½ kθ (θ − θ0)²
Udihedral = Σdihedrals kφ [1 + cos(nφ − δ)]
Unonbonded = Σi<j [ 4εij ( (σij/rij)¹² − (σij/rij)⁶ ) + qiqj / (4πε0εr rij) ]

This is the standard "Class I" additive force field form (AMBER, CHARMM, GROMOS, OPLS). Class II fields add cross-terms (bond-bond, bond-angle, etc.) and anharmonic potentials (Morse, cubic).

Long-Range Electrostatics

The 1/r Coulomb potential requires special treatment in periodic systems. The standard is Particle Mesh Ewald (PME): 

Uelec = Ureal + Urecip + Uself + Ucorrection

Real space: short-range damped Coulomb (cutoff ~1.0–1.2 nm). Reciprocal space: FFT on grid (spacing ~0.12 nm). PME is O(N log N) and essential for correct electrostatics. Reaction-field and cutoff methods are obsolete for production.

Lennard-Jones: Usually cut off at 1.0–1.2 nm with a long-range dispersion correction (energy/pressure). Force-switch or potential-switch functions avoid discontinuities.

Polarizable & Reactive Force Fields

Polarizable FF (AMOEBA, Drude, CHARMM-Drude) add induced dipoles: μind = α Elocal. Cost ~3–5× additive FF. Essential for: ion channels, metalloproteins, highly charged systems, interfaces.

Reactive FF (ReaxFF, AIREBO) allow bond breaking/formation via bond-order potentials. Cost ~10–100× classical. Used for combustion, fracture, catalysis. Not for routine biomolecular MD.

Coarse-grained (Martini 3, SIRAH, MARTINI) map 3–4 heavy atoms to 1 bead. Time step 20–40 fs. Enables μs–ms and μm scales. Loses atomic detail; requires backmapping for analysis.
