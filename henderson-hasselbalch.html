Exact Solution: The Full Equilibrium

The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation is an approximation. The exact pH of a buffer comes from solving the full system: mass balance, charge balance, and equilibrium constants.

For a buffer with analytical concentrations CHA and CA (added salt): 

[HA] + [A⁻] = CHA + CA = Ctot
[H⁺] + [Na⁺] = [OH⁻] + [A⁻]    (charge balance, assuming Na⁺ from NaA salt)
Kₐ = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]    Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻]

Eliminating [A⁻] and [HA] gives the exact cubic in [H⁺]: 

[H⁺]³ + (Kₐ + CA)[H⁺]² + (KₐCA − KₐCHA − Kw)[H⁺] − KₐKw = 0

The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation emerges when [H⁺], [OH⁺] ≪ CHA, CA (the "buffer approximation"). This fails for: very dilute buffers, extreme pH, or when pKₐ is near 7 (water autoionization matters).

Activity-Based Formulation

The thermodynamically rigorous form uses activities: 

pH = pKₐ° + log( [A⁻]/[HA] ) + log( γA⁻/γHA )

For neutral HA, γHA ≈ 1. For A⁻, γA⁻ depends on ionic strength I. Using the Davies equation at 25°C (A = 0.509): 

log γA⁻ = −0.509 ( √I/(1+√I) − 0.3I )

At I = 0.1 M, log γA⁻ ≈ −0.11 → pH is ~0.11 units lower than the concentration-based H-H prediction. This correction is essential for quantitative work in biological/environmental systems.

Buffer Preparation: Target pH → Composition

Given target pH and total concentration Ctot, the required ratio is: 

r = [A⁻]/[HA] = 10pH − pKₐ°
[HA] = Ctot / (1 + r)    [A⁻] = r Ctot / (1 + r)

In practice, one prepares by titration: start with the acid (or base) form, add strong base (or acid) while monitoring pH with a calibrated electrode. This automatically accounts for activity, temperature, and impurities.

For polyprotic systems (e.g., phosphate), prepare at the desired pH using the relevant pKₐ pair (pKₐ₂ = 7.20 for H₂PO₄⁻/HPO₄²⁻). The other protonation states are present at negligible levels if pH is within ±1.5 of the working pKₐ.

Non-Ideal Effects: Specific Ion Interactions

Beyond Debye–Hückel, specific ion interactions (e.g., Na⁺–HPO₄²⁻ ion pairing) can shift apparent pKₐ. The Pitzer equations or Specific Ion Interaction Theory (SIT) model these: 

log γA⁻ = −A z²√I/(1+√I) + Σ εij mj

where εij are interaction coefficients. For phosphate buffers, Na⁺–HPO₄²⁻ pairing is significant at > 0.1 M, causing the apparent pKₐ₂ to increase by ~0.05 units at 0.5 M NaCl.
