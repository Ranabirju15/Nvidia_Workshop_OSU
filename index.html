What is Molecular Dynamics?

Molecular Dynamics (MD) is a computer simulation method for analyzing the physical movements of atoms and molecules. The atoms and molecules are allowed to interact for a fixed period of time, giving a view of the dynamic evolution of the system. From these trajectories, we compute macroscopic observables via statistical mechanics.

The core idea: solve Newton's equations of motion for a system of N particles: 

mi d²ri/dt² = Fi = −∇i U(r1,...,rN)

where U is the potential energy function (force field), and Fi is the force on particle i. The trajectory {ri(t), vi(t)} generates a microcanonical (NVE) ensemble; other ensembles require thermostats/barostats.

The femtosecond time step is dictated by the fastest motions (bond vibrations involving hydrogen). Constraints (SHAKE, LINCS) allow 2–4 fs steps. Specialized hardware (Anton, GPUs) reaches μs–ms for biomolecules.

The MD Workflow

  1. System preparation — Structure acquisition (PDB, homology modeling), protonation states, solvation, ion placement.
  2. Force field assignment — Atom types, parameters, topology generation.
  3. Energy minimization — Remove bad contacts (steepest descent, conjugate gradient, L-BFGS).
  4. Equilibration — NVT then NPT phases; gradually release restraints; monitor T, P, density, energy.
  5. Production run — Unrestrained simulation in target ensemble; collect trajectory data.
  6. Analysis — RMSD, RMSF, RDF, hydrogen bonds, free energies, conformational clustering, etc.

Key Approximations & Limitations

  • Classical nuclei — No nuclear quantum effects (zero-point energy, tunneling). Path-integral MD addresses this.
  • Born–Oppenheimer approximation — Electronic structure not explicitly treated; force field is a parametric fit.
  • Force field accuracy — Fixed charges, no polarization (in standard FF), transferability limits.
  • Finite size & periodicity — Artifacts from PBC, minimum image convention, Ewald summation errors.
  • Sampling — Ergodicity breaking; rare events (folding, binding) often inaccessible on standard MD timescales.
